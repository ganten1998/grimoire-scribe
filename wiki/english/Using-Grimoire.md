# Using Grimoire

## The Library

Notebooks appear as covers with thumbnails, in folders that nest. A folder moved or renamed onto
one that is already there asks: merge, keep both or cancel. Tap the `...` on a notebook to rename,
move or delete it. Deleted notebooks go to `grimoire/trash`, so nothing is erased outright.

**New** makes a notebook or a folder. **From a file...** imports a PDF or pictures (see
[Importing](Importing)). **Share** opens the export page (see [Getting Pages Off the
Kindle](Exporting)). Two fingers swiped down over the Library open the Graph of all notebooks.

## The keyboard

Names can have accents: the **áé** key opens a page of accented letters (enough for Spanish,
Portuguese, French, Italian, German, Dutch, Catalan, Danish, Norwegian, Swedish and Finnish).
Shift gives the next letter its capital; tap it twice for caps on (a bar under the key's label,
every letter a capital until a third tap) and **abc** returns to the letters. With Grimoire in
Spanish, the letters page has **ñ** too, after l.

In Japanese that key is **あ**, a phone's flick pad: a tap types the row's first kana and a swipe
left, up, right or down the rest. **゛小** cycles the kana typed last (か to が, は to ば to ぱ, つ
to づ to っ), **カ** switches to katakana and **、** holds 、。？！ on its flicks.

**Delete held down** deletes one character after another on every keyboard (names, Find, the page
number pads, the count pad): a tap deletes one, and a finger or the pen kept on the key deletes the
next after about 0.4 s, then about 15 a second until it lifts or slides off. The count pad's - and
+ count on the same way, up to its limits.

**The caret**: every keyboard but the count pad has **◀** and **▶** at the right end of its text
line, which move the caret a letter (held, one after another, as delete does). A tap on the text
puts the caret there and a drag across it selects. Typing and delete act at the caret, and replace
or take a selection. The key beside them opens the edit page: **Select all**, **Cut**, **Copy**,
**Paste**, and **Start** and **End** for the caret. What you cut or copy stays until Grimoire
closes and pastes into any keyboard; it is apart from the lasso's ink. With a keyboard attached
(and shortcuts enabled), the arrows, Home and End move the caret (with Shift, they select) and
Ctrl or Cmd with A, C, X and V select all, copy, cut and paste.

## Paper

Narrow, medium and wide rule (without or with a margin), Cornell, dot grid, graph, sheet music,
guitar tab, blank, cross grid, isometric, hexagon, Seyes rule, and **Custom music**: stack treble,
bass, alto, tenor, drum and plain staves, 4 to 8 line tabs and lyric lines (or start from grand
staff or a 4 to 7 string bass tab), and choose how many systems fit on a page. Each page can have
its own paper.

Templates and photos can become a page's paper; see [Importing](Importing).

## Pens

Pencil, mechanical pencil, charcoal, ballpoint, fineliner, marker, fountain pen, calligraphy pen
and highlighter. Hold a pen on the toolbar for its sizes: a card of four widths in millimetres
(the eraser's also has Whole strokes / Precise). Every pen's pressure and tilt response can be
tuned in **Settings > Pen sensitivity**. The eraser takes away whole strokes, or set to
**Precise** only what it touches; the eraser end of the Premium Pen works too.

- **Charcoal** is grainy and crumbly, darker the harder you press; lay the pen low and it shades
  broad and soft with the side of the stick.
- **The calligraphy pen** is a broad-edge nib that sits at 90 degrees to the way the pen leans,
  like a real one: lean toward the bottom right (a right hand) and you get the classic 45 degree
  italic, thick going down to the right and hairline going up; lean toward the bottom left (a left
  hand) and the nib mirrors. Hold the pen upright and it keeps the 45 degree nib.

## The floating toolbar

The floating toolbar holds tools, undo/redo, clear page, pages, paper and the Library. Drag it
anywhere; tap the corner arrows to fold it into a small box. On the last page its next-page arrow
is a **+**: tap it for a new page, which opens (as swiping past the last page does). Hold it (or,
with a keyboard attached, tap it with Shift, Ctrl, Alt or Cmd held) for the number pad Pages uses
(at 1 at first): the pages are added after the page you are on, on its paper, which stays open,
and the Undo in Pages takes them back while they are still blank. The `new-pages` shortcut opens
the same pad on any page.

**Pen button**: hold the button while drawing to highlight (or erase; Settings). Its clicks and a
hold while hovering do nothing until you give them an action in **Settings > Gestures**. The
toolbar's broom clears the page: ink and outgoing links together (not a PDF's own links, its tabs);
Undo restores both. Cleared by a gesture or a shortcut, the page says so in a toast: "Page cleared.
Undo brings it back."

## The Index: Graph, List and Pages

Swipe down with two fingers for Graph, or tap the page counter for Pages. **Graph**, **List** and
**Pages** share one navigation bar; Back returns to the notebook.

- **Graph** always shows every linked page of every notebook, each notebook's pages an island with
  its name over it. A library with too many linked pages to draw at once shows this notebook alone,
  and says so. The view button at the bottom switches between **View: All** (the whole linked map)
  and **View: Adjacent** (the pages around one, and theirs); a tap switches and fits the view,
  after a pan or pinch too. With a keyboard the arrows pan, - and + zoom, Ctrl+0 fits, V switches
  the view, Ctrl+A selects every page drawn and Enter opens the selected page.
- **List** shows existing links.
- **Pages** has small cached previews and controls for adding, copying, selecting and deleting
  pages. Hold a page for its card (Move to..., Copy to notebook..., Rename, Delete), or hold it a
  moment (300 ms, before the card would open) and drag it off to a gap between two pages to move
  it (a black bar shows the gap; no card opens). Right after a move, the bottom row's Undo puts
  the pages back, and then its Redo moves them again. Tap **+** (New page) for one blank page at
  once, after the open page (or the last one ticked), on that page's paper; Pages stays open and
  the new page's tile says "(new)". Hold **+** for a number pad that asks how many; **Go to
  page...** opens the page-number-or-name sheet. With a keyboard, Ctrl+A ticks every page and
  Ctrl+N opens the number pad. The Undo button takes a page or batch back while its pages are
  still blank, and Redo adds the same pages again. While Pages is open, the `undo` and `redo`
  keys, controller buttons and gestures are Pages' own Undo and Redo.

**The Graph from the Library**: swipe down with two fingers over the Library (or press the key
bound to `index-map`). Tap a page twice to open it; Back from there, even from another notebook a
link led to, or the Links bar's **Back to Graph**, comes back to the Graph. Spread two fingers to
zoom. A library with too many linked pages opens the notebooks' browser instead, to pick one
notebook's Graph. A notebook's or a folder's menu has **Leave out of the Graph** / **Show in the
Graph**.

Swipe up with two fingers for the page-status panel: notebook name, page count, link areas and
**Name page** and **Go to page** over the notebook. Page names stay attached when pages move and
appear in the PDF table of contents. The toolbar's settings cog opens Settings; Done returns to
the notebook.

## Links

Tap the toolbar's link-plus button and draw around the area. Choose a page in this notebook, or
**Somewhere Else** to browse folders, pick another notebook and choose its page. The selector
starts in the current notebook's folder and shows cached page previews. Selection strokes leave no
ink; draw a visible border separately if desired.

A finger tap follows the link; hold it to change its destination, redraw its area or delete it.
Link edits have Undo/Redo. Notebook IDs and page IDs keep cross-notebook links attached through
renames, folder moves and page reordering. Ordinary erasing leaves links intact; Clear page
removes them with the ink. Existing links to the old Index remain valid and open the unified
Index.

**Document links**: the tabs of a planner PDF are part of its pages. A tap follows one (a link you
drew yourself comes first where they overlap); a finger held on one starts Select. Clear page
keeps them, and Copy page, Move to... and Copy to notebook... carry them. The Links bar lists
them as one row, "From the PDF (N)", which opens them grouped by the page they go to. They live in
`doclinks.ini`, apart from your own links.

## Live view

**Library > Share > Live view** shows what Grimoire shows, live, in a computer's browser on the
same Wi-Fi: open the address it gives and type its code (or scan its QR code with a phone).

A slim rail beside the page holds the controls: **Size** (Fit, Fit width, 25% to 200%; Ctrl or Cmd
with the scroll wheel, a trackpad pinch, or the - + 0 keys), **Picture in picture**, Monitor mode,
full screen, recording, **View settings** (transparent paper, colors, warmth and brightness, the
pen dot, the toolbar and clock layers, latency) and **Keys**. Picture in picture floats the page
over your other windows; keep the original tab open meanwhile, and close the small window to bring
the view back. Grimoire's toasts show in the browser too.

A small icon in the top right corner says it is on; **Stop** on its card turns it off, and it
turns itself off after 10 minutes with nobody watching. Plain HTTP: use it on a network you trust.

## Wi-Fi networks

**Settings > Wi-Fi** turns Wi-Fi on or off, says which network the Kindle is connected to, and
lists the networks the Kindle has saved in their own order. The status line names the connected
network. Tap one to switch: "Connecting to ..." until connected, or "Could not connect to ..."
when it refuses or has not connected in 45 seconds. Switching disconnects Share, Live view and SSH
from the network you leave.

Add a network or change its password in the Kindle's settings or KOReader: no Wi-Fi password
reaches the app, its files or its log (its helper keeps only the networks' names), and no network
is added, changed or forgotten.

## Clock and battery

Enabled by default, small and at the top left. It shows the time and a small battery with its
percent (a bolt over it while it charges), in a box over the page; tap the corner to hide or show
it. It shows on the notebook page only, never goes into a page, a PDF or Live view, and waits while
the pen is near. **Settings > Display** sets its size and which corner it sits in.

## Zoom and rotation

Pinch to zoom up to 4x, or inward to return to the fitted page view. Grimoire turns with the
Kindle (switchable).

## Sleep and leaving

**Sleep**: the power button sleeps the Kindle *inside* Grimoire, showing your page (or a pinned
page, or the Kindle's own screensaver). Waking brings you straight back.

**Leaving**: **Library > Exit**, or hold the power button 4 seconds on any screen, asleep or
awake. Grimoire saves your page and closes, and the Kindle comes back. If the page cannot be saved
(the Kindle's storage is full), a hold while awake says so and stays; hold again within a minute
to leave without it. Opened from KOReader, Exit reads **Back to KOReader**, and both ways out take
you back to KOReader.

## Passcode

Started from the Kindle Library, Grimoire leaves your Device Passcode on. When the Kindle goes to
sleep, Grimoire saves your page and hands the screen and touch input to the Kindle, so its own
keypad can ask for the passcode; when the Kindle reports the unlock, Grimoire takes them back and
shows your notebook. With a passcode set, the keypad comes before a custom sleep picture, and the
Kindle interface keeps running for it.

Confirmed upright and sideways on the Scribe 2024 with firmware 5.20.1.0.2; other firmware still
needs a check. KOReader normally turns the passcode off for its whole session, and Grimoire
opened from KOReader leaves it that way. Start Grimoire from the Library when you need the
passcode.

## White ink, the eraser and pinned pens

- **White ink**: tap the pen button on the toolbar; the picker's bottom row is **Black | White**.
  With White, every pen draws white with its own texture, taking ink away while the paper's lines
  stay. The pen in use shows a hollow icon while white is on.
- **The eraser**: **Pen feel > the eraser's tab > Eraser** is **Whole strokes** (a touch takes the
  whole stroke) or **Precise** (it takes away only what it touches). Tapping the eraser again goes
  back to the pen you used before it. Settings' pen-button row can also make holding the pen
  button draw white. The Pen feel test pad starts with handwritten "erase me!" ink; **Reset pad**
  restores it, and **Reset tool** restores the tuned widths and Whole strokes mode.
- **Fine pen adjustments**: in Pen feel, hold a slider briefly, then move above or below its track
  while keeping contact. The farther away you move, the smaller each horizontal adjustment
  becomes. Move back within a knob-height band around the track for the knob to catch up and
  follow directly again. Release to keep the value; the next press starts normally.
- **Pinned pens**: in the picker, tap **Pin**, then the pens you want on the toolbar (up to
  three), then Done. Each gets its own button under the pen button. From another tool, including
  the eraser, tap the top pen button once to select its pen and again to open the picker. If the
  button shows a grid, it opens the picker directly.
